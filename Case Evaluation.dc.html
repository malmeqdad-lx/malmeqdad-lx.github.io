Catching the responses that are correct and still wrong

Accuracy metrics pass a response that gives the right number in the wrong register. A client asking about a loss does not want a cheerful confirmation, and no factuality check will flag one. This is the layer that does.

Role

Rubric design, reviewer calibration, golden set construction, model selection.

Scale

Over a million conversations a month, reviewed systemically for recurring failure patterns.

Model bake-off

Five providers scored against golden sets built from production conversations.

The gap

Deterministic metrics answer deterministic questions. Did it route correctly, did it disambiguate, did it retrieve the right document. Those are necessary and they are also easy, in the sense that a disagreement between two reviewers can be settled by looking.

The failures that reach a complaint are rarely factual. They are responses that answer the literal question and miss the act being performed — a request for reassurance handled as a request for data, an apology delivered in the register of a receipt. Speech-act theory gives that a vocabulary, and a vocabulary is what makes it scoreable rather than a matter of taste.

Reviewers who disagree by more than one point on any dimension resolve it against the anchor examples, not against each other. That conversation is the calibration; the rubric is just where it gets written down.

What it was for

Choosing a model on more than benchmarks

Five providers, scored against golden sets built from real production conversations rather than public evaluation suites. The winner was not the strongest model on paper; it was the one that stayed in character under the client's own edge cases.

Stress-testing a voice channel before launch

Production transcripts mined for realistic scenario distributions, then run as Monte Carlo simulations against the new channel. The point was to meet the improbable-but-real calls in a test harness rather than on day one.

Turning failure patterns into a design queue

A systemic review across product, science and engineering, where the output was not a report but a prioritised list of prompt and flow changes, with the evidence for each one attached.

Position

A rubric that two reviewers score differently is not a rubric. Most of the work in evaluation is not deciding what good looks like — it is writing it down precisely enough that other people arrive at the same answer without you in the room.
